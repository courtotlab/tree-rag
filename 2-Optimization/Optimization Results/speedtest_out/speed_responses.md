### Q: What are the Ten Steps To CAP Laboratory Accreditation?
`steps=40 calls=41`

**The College of American Pathologists (CAP) outlines a ten‑step process for a laboratory to become accredited.**  
The steps are taken in order, from the initial request for an application through ongoing self‑inspection after the laboratory receives its certificate.

| Step | What the laboratory does |
|------|---------------------------|
| **1 – Request Application** | Fill out and submit the *Request for Application* form (available at cap.org). International labs must have been enrolled in CAP proficiency testing/EQA for at least six months before requesting an application. [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **2 – Review Welcome Kit** | Check the email that contains the link to the online application (“CAP Accreditation Application Available”). Review the additional information that comes with the link and, if desired, schedule an optional onboarding call with CAP staff. [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **3 – Complete Application** | Log in to the e‑LAB Solutions Suite, complete the accreditation application within three months of its availability, watch for email reminders, and work with CAP staff on any follow‑up questions. [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **4 – Receive Customized Checklists** | Receive (by mail or download) the CAP‑generated checklists that are specific to the laboratory and begin inspection preparation. [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **5 – Schedule Inspection Date** | Arrange a date for the peer‑based inspection (the guide instructs laboratories to look for the scheduling information after the checklists are received). [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **6 – Inspection Preparation & Conduct** | Using the customized checklists, prepare for and undergo the on‑site CAP inspection. (Steps 4‑6 are described collectively as “inspection prep and conduct” in the guide.) [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **7 – Respond to Deficiencies (within 30 days)** | After the inspection, review any deficiency letters, compile all responses, and submit them within 30 calendar days of the inspection date. [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **8 – Support CAP Review of Responses** | Work with the CAP technical specialist to answer follow‑up questions, provide any additional documentation requested, and await the accreditation decision (typically within 75 days of the inspection). [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **9 – Receive Certificate of Accreditation** | The laboratory’s Certificate of Accreditation is mailed to the laboratory director; the initial inspection date becomes the laboratory’s accreditation anniversary. [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |
| **10 – Perform Self‑Inspection & Maintain Continuous Compliance** | On the anniversary date, conduct a self‑inspection using CAP‑provided materials, update any changes (directorship, location, test menu, personnel, etc.) in the e‑LAB Solutions Suite, and enroll or discontinue PT/EQA products as needed. [[folders/Management/Accreditation/CAP/2018-guide-to-accreditation.pdf]] |

These ten steps constitute the complete CAP laboratory accreditation pathway as described in the **2018 Guide to CAP Accreditation**.

### Q: What is the minimum concentration of cfDNA aliquot needed for pWGS?
`steps=13 calls=14`

The assay was validated down to the lowest input tested – 1 ng of cfDNA.  In the input‑quantity experiments the library‑preparation metrics (mapped reads, insert size, duplicate rate, coverage per Gb, tumour‑fraction estimate) were all acceptable at the 1 ng level, and the validation report states that the assay “was validated with inputs as low as 1 ng of cfDNA”【folders/Assay_Validation/Approved Validation Reports/Plasma Whole Genome Sequencing Validation Report v3.0.pdf】.  

Thus, the minimum amount of cfDNA required for plasma whole‑genome sequencing (pWGS) is **1 ng** (the corresponding concentration would depend on the volume of the aliquot, but the required input mass is 1 ng).

The Illumina NextSeq 2000 workflow requires the following reagents (and consumables) [folders/Technical SOPs and Worksheets/NextSeq 2000 Operation and Maintenance.docx]:

- **P1 kit** – primary reagent cartridge for the run
